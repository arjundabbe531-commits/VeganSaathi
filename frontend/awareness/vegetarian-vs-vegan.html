Vegetarian vs Vegan — Not the Same Thing

"Vegetarian" and "vegan" are often used loosely as if one is just a stricter version of the other. They are actually different rule-sets, and in an Indian context the gap between them is unusually wide.

TermExcludesIncludes
Vegetarian (lacto-vegetarian, India's default)Meat, fish, poultry, eggsDairy
EggetarianMeat, fish, poultryEggs, dairy
JainMeat, fish, poultry, eggs, root/underground vegetables, usually honeyDairy, above-ground vegetables, grains, pulses
VeganAll animal-derived products — meat, fish, poultry, eggs, dairy, honeyOnly plant-derived foods

Why "vegetarian" doesn't mean "vegan-safe" in India

Indian vegetarian cooking relies heavily on dairy — ghee as a cooking fat, curd as a daily staple, paneer as a default protein, and milk sweets at festivals. That makes a dish or restaurant labelled "pure veg" very likely to still contain dairy, and therefore not automatically safe for a vegan eater. VeganSaathi tracks vegan status as its own explicit tag on every place, rather than assuming vegetarian listings are vegan-friendly by default.

Jain is its own system, not "extra strict vegetarian"

Jain dietary rules follow from the principle of non-violence extended to plants and micro-organisms — root vegetables like onion, garlic, and potato are avoided because uprooting them is believed to disturb the whole plant and surrounding soil life, not because of taste or tradition alone. Practice varies by individual and sect.
