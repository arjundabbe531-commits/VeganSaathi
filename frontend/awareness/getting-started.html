Getting Started: A Guide for Students

Practical, budget-friendly, and non-judgmental steps for hostel residents and college students exploring plant-based meals.

Adopting plant-based habits does not require buying expensive imported ingredients or overhauling your life overnight. As a college student at SSGMCE or living in Shegaon, the most sustainable approach is pragmatic, gradual, and rooted in the foods you already know and enjoy.

1. Start with Familiar Foods

Much of traditional Indian cuisine is naturally plant-based. Before seeking out specialized substitutes, take advantage of the diverse dishes already available in campus canteens and local eateries:

  • Breakfast: Kanda poha garnished with roasted peanuts and fresh lemon; idli-sambar with coconut chutney; vegetable upma cooked in sunflower oil.
  • Lunch & Dinner: Dal tadka with steamed rice; chana masala or rajma with tawa rotis (request without ghee); aloo bhaji or baingan bharta; sprouted moong usal.
  • Snacks: Bhelpuri (confirm no sev fried in ghee), roasted makhana, boiled sweet corn, roasted peanuts with jaggery.

2. Navigating the Hostel Mess

Hostel dining presents shared catering routines, but small, polite customizations go a long way:

  • Simple requests: Ask the mess staff: "Bhaiya, meri roti pe ghee ya butter mat lagana" (Please don't apply ghee/butter to my roti). Most mess staff are happy to hand you dry rotis directly from the tawa.
  • Check daily gravies: Ask if the day's special sabzi contains cream (malai) or paneer. If so, double down on the dal, sukhi sabzi (dry vegetable), and rice.
  • Breakfast swaps: When boiled milk or tea with milk is served, choose warm water, lemon water, or black tea/coffee.

3. The Student Hostel Room Pantry

Keep affordable, non-perishable whole plant foods in your hostel room for quick study breaks and late-night hunger:

Roasted Chana & Peanuts: Inexpensive, shelf-stable, high in protein and healthy fats.
Sattu (Roasted Gram Flour): Mix with water, lemon juice, and roasted cumin for an instant protein beverage.
Rolled Oats & Bananas: Great for quick breakfasts; soak with hot water, raisins, and a spoonful of peanut butter.
Soya Chunks: Readily available in local grocery stores across Shegaon; extremely cost-effective protein for mess meals.

4. Key Nutritional Awareness: Vitamin B12 & Iron

Nutritional awareness ensures you feel energetic, sharp, and healthy during exams and semester workloads:

  • Vitamin B12 is essential: Harvard Health notes that vitamin B12 is found naturally only in animal products. The NHS advises that sources for vegans are limited, so a vitamin B12 supplement may be needed. Fortified foods or a supplement (cyanocobalamin or methylcobalamin) are the usual options. Consult a doctor or medical officer at the campus health center.
  • Iron Absorption: Plant-derived (non-haem) iron found in spinach, dal, and chana is absorbed significantly better when paired with Vitamin C. Squeeze fresh lemon juice over your dal, salads, or sprouts to maximize absorption naturally.
