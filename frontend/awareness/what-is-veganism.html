What is Veganism?

Veganism is generally defined as a way of living that seeks to exclude, as far as is possible and practicable, all forms of exploitation of and cruelty to animals — for food, clothing, or any other purpose. In dietary terms, this means avoiding all products derived wholly or partly from animals: meat, fish, poultry, eggs, dairy, and honey.

Veganism is usually described as an ethical stance first and a diet second. Many people who avoid animal products day-to-day describe themselves more loosely as "plant-based," which overlaps heavily with veganism but is often motivated primarily by health rather than an ethical framework. VeganSaathi's food-discovery features are useful to both groups regardless of motivation — we don't assume everyone shares (or needs to share) the same reason for their diet.

Why this matters in an Indian context

India has one of the world's largest vegetarian populations, but Indian vegetarian cooking is often dairy-heavy — ghee, curd, paneer, and khoya are structural to many dishes, not optional garnishes. That makes India, by many accounts, an easy place to eat vegetarian and a genuinely harder place to eat strictly vegan. See Vegetarian vs Vegan for more on this distinction.
